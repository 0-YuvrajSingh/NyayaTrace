[PARAGRAPH] Executive Summary
[PARAGRAPH] This project studies how Artificial Intelligence can assist legal research in the Indian legal system while remaining explainable, evidence-grounded, verifiable, and subject to human control. The proposed system is an Explainable Legal Research and Decision-Support Framework rather than an autonomous judge or lawyer.
[PARAGRAPH] The primary task is legal research and evidence-backed case analysis: given a legal question or case facts, the system retrieves relevant statutes and precedents, selects supporting passages, verifies citation identity and provenance, and produces a structured explanation that a legal professional can inspect. Court-outcome prediction is retained as a secondary, measurable capability rather than the identity of the project.
[PARAGRAPH] Core research question: Can an evidence-grounded and provenance-constrained AI framework improve the reliability and explainability of Indian legal research compared with an AI workflow that is not explicitly grounded in verified legal evidence?
[PARAGRAPH] The project will use Indian legal cases and associated authority information, with ILDC as the principal academic benchmark where applicable. The system will combine a traditional benchmark, a legal-language baseline, BM25 retrieval, evidence selection, citation/provenance tracking, a controlled generation layer where feasible, and a structured explanation surface. Temporal eligibility of legal evidence will be implemented as a reliability constraint for historical evaluation, but it is not the primary novelty claim. Multilingual support is explicitly deferred to future work.
[PARAGRAPH] This document is the frozen semester scope. It prioritizes a defensible research contribution, measurable reliability constraints, and a reproducible implementation over feature breadth.
[PARAGRAPH] Problem
[PARAGRAPH] Legal AI introduces a reliability problem that differs from ordinary text-generation systems. A fluent model can produce a convincing legal explanation while citing an incorrect case, inventing a paragraph, relying on evidence that was not retrieved, or presenting a weak authority as if it were decisive. These failures are particularly serious in law because a material claim must be inspectable against a legal source.
[PARAGRAPH] The Indian context makes this concrete. In Pooja Ramesh Singh v. Jammu and Kashmir Bank Ltd. (2026 INSC 668), the Supreme Court addressed reliance on AI-generated fake and hallucinated case law and the consequences for judicial decision-making. The judgment provides a current motivation for verification, provenance, and human oversight. [1]
[PARAGRAPH] The project therefore treats explainability as a system property, not as a cosmetic paragraph added after generation. A useful output must connect material claims to retrieved evidence and preserve enough provenance for a reviewer to reproduce the source path.
[PARAGRAPH] Research Gap
[PARAGRAPH] Prior Indian legal-NLP work has already demonstrated court judgment prediction and explanation. ILDC introduced a large Indian Supreme Court corpus and a Court Judgment Prediction and Explanation task, establishing that legal prediction and explanation are feasible but not equivalent to expert legal reasoning. [2]
[PARAGRAPH] The proposed project does not claim novelty for Legal-BERT, BM25, RAG, court judgment prediction, or explainability individually. The research gap targeted here is the integration and controlled evaluation of multiple reliability constraints in an Indian legal research workflow: evidence grounding, citation/provenance restriction, temporal eligibility for historical evaluation, and a legally meaningful explanation surface.
[PARAGRAPH] The contribution is therefore a measurable framework and experimental study rather than a claim that the underlying AI techniques are new.
[PARAGRAPH] Final Contribution
[PARAGRAPH] The project will contribute a reproducible Indian legal XAI framework that produces a research output only after retrieving relevant authorities, selecting evidence from that retrieval set, attaching provenance metadata, validating citation identity/source path, and generating a structured explanation of the evidence used. Outcome prediction is evaluated as one capability of the framework, not as the sole system objective.

[PARAGRAPH] Research Questions
[PARAGRAPH] Only the following three research questions are in scope.
[PARAGRAPH] RQ1 — Does grounding an Indian legal AI workflow in retrieved legal evidence improve legal-research reliability and relevant-evidence retrieval compared with a facts-only legal-language baseline?
[PARAGRAPH] RQ2 — Does provenance-constrained evidence selection and citation verification reduce unsupported or unverifiable legal claims in the final output?
[PARAGRAPH] RQ3 — Can the proposed structured explanation format improve human-verifiable transparency without materially degrading prediction or retrieval performance?
[PARAGRAPH] Hypotheses
[PARAGRAPH] H1 — Evidence-grounded retrieval will improve relevant-evidence retrieval and will provide a stronger basis for legal-research outputs than a facts-only legal-language baseline.
[PARAGRAPH] H2 — Provenance-constrained citation and verification will reduce unsupported or unverifiable citations compared with an unconstrained generation/evidence-selection condition.
[PARAGRAPH] H3 — Structured, evidence-linked explanations will receive higher human-rated transparency and verifiability than outputs that provide a conclusion without an explicit evidence path, subject to reviewer availability.
[PARAGRAPH] Research Objectives
[PARAGRAPH] Develop a reproducible AI pipeline for Indian legal case analysis using a legal-language model and an explicit legal retrieval layer.
[PARAGRAPH] Ground material legal claims in retrieved legal passages rather than relying only on model-internal knowledge.
[PARAGRAPH] Implement citation provenance so every final cited authority can be traced to a concrete source document and passage.
[PARAGRAPH] Implement temporal eligibility as a historical-evaluation integrity constraint so later legal information does not enter historical test cases.
[PARAGRAPH] Generate structured explanations showing the issue, authority, evidence, and conclusion path without exposing private model chain-of-thought.
[PARAGRAPH] Evaluate retrieval quality, citation correctness, provenance, temporal integrity, explanation quality, and secondary outcome-prediction performance.
[PARAGRAPH] Define governance controls for human oversight, privacy, auditability, contestability, and non-autonomous use.
[PARAGRAPH] Operational Definitions
[PARAGRAPH] The following definitions formalize concepts already required elsewhere in this document (Temporal Integrity Protocol, Citation and Provenance Protocol, Evaluation Metrics). They clarify measurement so the project does not move its rules after seeing results; they introduce no new technical requirement.
[TABLE] Term | Operational definition
[TABLE] Temporal existence | An authority/document is available in the corpus at or before the relevant historical time.
[TABLE] Temporal effectiveness | A statute/provision is treated as effective only during its defined validity interval when reliable metadata is available.
[TABLE] Temporal applicability | Whether the frozen historical-eligibility rule (Eligible(e,t)) permits the item for the evaluation case.
[TABLE] Provenance validity | The final citation maps to an identified source document and exact passage that was retrieved for the case/query. This is the same quantity already named “Citation Provenance Validity” in the Evaluation Metrics table below.
[TABLE] Authority consistency | The cited authority matches the predefined reference evidence/authority mapping used for evaluation. This is the same quantity already measured by “Authority-consistent Precision/Recall/F1” below.
[PARAGRAPH] These definitions must be frozen before final test evaluation and may not be changed after inspecting results.

[PARAGRAPH] E1–E4 Experimental Core
[PARAGRAPH] Four experiments provide an incremental and interpretable evaluation. The primary research question is answered by the retrieval/evidence/explanation comparisons; E1 and E2 establish predictive baselines, and outcome prediction remains secondary.
[TABLE] Experiment | Method | Input | Purpose
[TABLE] E1 — Traditional Baseline | TF-IDF + Logistic Regression | Case facts/text | Establish a simple, reproducible outcome-prediction benchmark.
[TABLE] E2 — Legal-Language Baseline | Legal-BERT + case facts | Case facts only | Measure performance without explicit retrieval grounding.
[TABLE] E3 — Retrieval-Grounded Legal Research | Legal-BERT or controlled generator + BM25 evidence | Query/case facts + top-k retrieved legal passages | Measure whether explicit legal evidence improves retrieval-grounded research output; prediction is reported as a secondary measure.
[TABLE] E4 — Proposed Explainable Framework | E3 + provenance-constrained selection + citation validation + structured explanation + temporal integrity | Case facts/query + selected evidence + provenance metadata | Test whether reliability and explainability constraints improve evidence quality and transparency without unacceptable performance loss.
[PARAGRAPH] E3 and E4 must use the same data splits, preprocessing, retrieval index, top-k, model configuration, and random seeds wherever applicable. The controlled change is the evidence-control, verification, temporal-integrity, and explanation layer.
[PARAGRAPH] Temporal Integrity Protocol
[PARAGRAPH] Temporal eligibility is included as an integrity control for historical evaluation, not as the primary novelty claim. A historical case should not be evaluated using later legal information when the experimental question is what the system could have used at the case decision time.
[PARAGRAPH] Statute passages should carry effective and end-validity metadata where available.
[PARAGRAPH] Precedent eligibility should require decision_date <= case_date, with the target case and duplicates/near-duplicates excluded.
[PARAGRAPH] Unresolved temporal metadata must follow a predefined rule and must not be manually corrected after observing test results.
[PARAGRAPH] Current-law deployment is conceptually different from historical prediction and is not a separate semester benchmark.
[PARAGRAPH] Explainability Framework
[PARAGRAPH] Explainability is defined in legal terms rather than only as model-internal feature attribution. The system should answer four questions for a reviewer: what did the system output, which evidence supports it, why was that evidence selected, and where exactly can the reviewer verify it.
[TABLE] Explanation layer | What the user sees | Measurement
[TABLE] Evidence linkage | Exact retrieved passage associated with a material claim | Evidence-grounding rate / citation match
[TABLE] Authority metadata | Court, case/Act name, section/citation, date, authority type | Authority-consistency P/R/F1
[TABLE] Selection rationale | Relevance score/rank plus short structured reason | Retrieval metrics; qualitative review
[TABLE] Model attribution | Important text spans/features for the prediction where technically feasible | Attribution stability/faithfulness checks
[TABLE] Structured legal view | Issue -> Authority/Rule -> Evidence -> Conclusion | Human-rated explanation quality
[TABLE] Uncertainty | Confidence or limitation statement without implying legal certainty | Calibration/uncertainty analysis if feasible
[PARAGRAPH] Safety boundary: the system will not expose hidden chain-of-thought or present private internal reasoning as authoritative legal reasoning. Explanations will be evidence- and output-focused.

[PARAGRAPH] Evaluation Metrics
[TABLE] Category | Metric | Purpose
[TABLE] Retrieval | Recall@5 / Recall@k | Whether relevant legal evidence appears in retrieved results
[TABLE] Citation | Authority-consistent Precision / Recall / F1 | Whether cited authorities match the predefined evaluation evidence
[TABLE] Grounding | Citation Groundedness Rate | Fraction of material cited claims linked to retrieved passages
[TABLE] Integrity | Temporal Violation Rate | Frequency of temporally ineligible evidence entering historical output
[TABLE] Provenance | Citation Provenance Validity | Fraction of final citations traceable to a specific retrieved source/passage
[TABLE] Explanation | Human-rated explanation quality | Whether reviewers can understand and verify the evidence basis
[TABLE] Reliability | Unsupported-claim rate | Material legal claims that lack retrievable support
[TABLE] Prediction | Accuracy; Macro-F1 | Secondary outcome-prediction performance
[TABLE] Efficiency | Latency / memory / compute | Practical feasibility of the research prototype
[PARAGRAPH] Note on scope: Temporal Violation Rate is the sole required temporal integrity metric. Named sub-decompositions (e.g., separately reporting retrieved-set exposure versus final-citation exposure, or a prediction delta across E3/E4) were reviewed and are explicitly not adopted as additional required metrics, since they are not part of the approved baseline. Nothing prevents the implementation team from computing such breakdowns internally for debugging, but they must not be reported as frozen evaluation deliverables or used to imply a broader evaluation objective than the one specified here.
[PARAGRAPH] Evaluation Reference Evidence
[PARAGRAPH] Retrieval and citation evaluation must use a predefined reference evidence set or defensible authority mapping available before final test evaluation. The reference must not be constructed retrospectively from the system’s own retrieved output. If existing ILDC authority/explanation annotations are used, the mapping procedure must be documented and frozen before final scoring. [2]
[PARAGRAPH] Mandatory Error Analysis
[PARAGRAPH] E2 wrong, E3/E4 correct: evidence retrieval contributed useful information.
[PARAGRAPH] E3 correct, E4 wrong: evidence constraints or selection removed useful support.
[PARAGRAPH] E3/E4 prediction correct but citation unsupported: prediction and evidence quality diverge.
[PARAGRAPH] Correct authority retrieved but final answer wrong: retrieval was sufficient but reasoning/prediction failed.
[PARAGRAPH] Citation is traceable but authority-consistency fails: provenance succeeded without proving legal relevance.
[PARAGRAPH] Citation is later than the historical case date: temporal integrity failure.
[PARAGRAPH] Explanation highlights text that does not correspond to the retrieved supporting evidence: explanation faithfulness failure.
[PARAGRAPH] Responsible AI and Governance Requirements
[PARAGRAPH] The system is a decision-support research prototype. It must be designed around human oversight, transparency, accountability, privacy, contestability, and non-autonomous use. NITI Aayog’s Responsible AI work identifies safety and reliability, equality, inclusivity/non-discrimination, privacy/security, transparency, accountability, and protection of positive human values as guiding principles. [3]
[PARAGRAPH] The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is a key privacy-law source for analysing personal-data handling in any deployment context, while its application and exemptions must be assessed against the specific use case rather than assumed. [4]
[TABLE] Control | Required treatment
[TABLE] Human oversight | Every consequential legal output is labelled as AI assistance and requires human verification.
[TABLE] No autonomous decision | No autonomous judicial, sentencing, bail, guilt, or final legal decision function.
[TABLE] Citation verification | A final output cannot introduce a legal authority outside the verified retrieval/provenance set.
[TABLE] Auditability | Log model version, retrieval configuration, evidence IDs, relevant input/output metadata, and experiment IDs.
[TABLE] Privacy | Minimize stored personal data; apply access control and redaction/pseudonymisation where appropriate.
[TABLE] Bias/fairness | Measure error patterns and subgroup differences when ethically and legally appropriate and when the dataset supports such evaluation.
[TABLE] Uncertainty | Do not equate model confidence with legal certainty; disclose limitations.
[TABLE] Contestability | Reviewer must be able to reject an evidence item, challenge an output, and inspect the source passage.

[PARAGRAPH] The research scope is frozen around the evidence-grounded, explainable legal AI framework for legal research and decision support. [PARAGRAPH] Final One-Paragraph Summary
[PARAGRAPH] This project proposes an Explainable AI Framework for Indian legal research in which a legal question or historical case is processed through a legal-language model and an explicit legal retrieval pipeline. BM25 retrieves relevant statutes and precedent passages; an evidence-selection layer chooses support; provenance metadata records the exact source and passage; citation validation rejects unsupported authorities; temporal eligibility prevents later information from entering historical evaluation; and an explanation layer exposes the evidence basis of the final output. The core evaluation compares a traditional predictive baseline, a facts-only legal-language baseline, a retrieval-grounded legal research system, and the full provenance-constrained explainable framework using retrieval, citation, grounding, temporal-integrity, provenance, explanation, and secondary outcome-prediction metrics. The project does not attempt to replace judges or lawyers, and multilingual support is intentionally deferred to future work.
